## 4:40 AM, Lohagadwadi, and Your Bag Is Already Too Heavy

The sky is still the colour of wet slate. A few trekkers are stamping their feet near a tea stall, holding paper cups with both hands. You are standing at the base of the trail with a backpack that feels fine for the first ten minutes and wrong after that.

Inside it: three bottles of cold drink, a full-size towel, a power bank you forgot to charge, one extra T-shirt that you will never wear, and sandals "just in case". What is not inside it: a torch, a rain-proof cover for your phone, a proper snack, and the one thing every experienced trekker in Pune never leaves home without, which is a plan for the descent.

This is not a rare situation. It is the most common start to a first trek near Pune, and it is entirely avoidable.

A weekend trek in the Sahyadris is one of the best low-cost plans a Pune resident can make. It costs very little, it clears your head, and it is the fastest way to meet people without forcing a conversation. But it works only when your preparation is sorted. This Pune weekend trek checklist covers what to wear, what to carry, what to leave behind, and how to choose a trail that matches your fitness on the day.

## Why Most Weekend Trek Problems Have Nothing to Do With Fitness

When people think about trekking trouble, they imagine exhaustion or a bad fall. In practice, the common issues on one-day treks near Pune are smaller and more irritating:

- Shoes with worn-out soles that slip on wet rock or loose laterite
- Cotton clothes that stay damp with sweat and turn cold at a windy plateau
- Not enough water, or too much water in the wrong bottles
- A phone that dies before the descent, taking your map, torch and cab app with it
- Starting late, so the climb happens in the harshest sun

None of these needs special training. All of them need a checklist. That is why we built one into how we brief first-time attendees at our adventure events, and it is why this guide is arranged the way it is: by what actually goes wrong, not by what looks impressive in a gear catalogue.

## Footwear and Clothing: Where Beginners Lose the Most Comfort

### The shoe decision

Your shoes decide whether the trek is enjoyable or merely survivable. A proper trekking shoe with a rubber outsole and a deep tread is ideal, but a decent pair of running shoes with good grip works for easy forts like Sinhagad or Lohagad. What does not work is a smooth-soled canvas shoe, a fashion sneaker, or anything you bought last week and have not walked in.

Break in new shoes for at least two short walks before the trek. A blister at the forty-minute mark cannot be negotiated with.

Sandals and floaters are tempting for the heat, but loose scree and wet steps near fort entrances punish open footwear. Leave them for the post-trek chai stop.

### Dress in layers, not in fashion

Maharashtra's hill sections change temperature quickly, especially after sunrise. For a typical winter trek:

1. **Base layer:** a dry-fit or polyester T-shirt that moves sweat away from the skin
2. **Mid layer:** a light fleece or full-sleeve top for the cold start
3. **Outer layer:** a thin windcheater that packs into your bag

Cotton looks harmless, but once it is soaked it stays wet and takes your body heat with it. Jeans are worse, since they stiffen, chafe and make high steps uncomfortable. Choose track pants or stretchable trekking trousers instead.

A cap and sunglasses help on exposed plateaus. A buff or bandana helps with dust and wind.

## The Backpack: What Goes In and What Stays Out

For a one-day trek, a 20 to 30 litre backpack with padded shoulder straps and a chest or waist strap is enough. The golden rule: if you can feel the weight at the parking area, take something out.

Pack heavy items close to your back and in the middle, light items at the top, and keep these within quick reach:

- Water bottle (side pocket)
- Snacks (top pocket)
- Phone and ID in a zip bag (front pocket)
- Torch and whistle (accessible without opening the main compartment)

Line the main compartment with a plastic bag or use a rain cover. Pune's weather is generally kind in winter, but Sahyadri mist and sudden drizzle are not unusual, and a wet bag ruins everything inside it.

## Water and Food: The Quiet Make-or-Break Items

### How much water is enough

A practical rule that most trek leaders follow is 1.5 to 2 litres for a half-day trek and more for longer or hotter routes. Start sipping early rather than waiting to feel thirsty, because by then you are already behind. If you are doing a fort with no water source at the top, treat your bottle count as non-negotiable.

Carry ORS or an electrolyte sachet, especially if you sweat a lot. Plain water alone does not always cover what you lose on a long climb.

### Snacks that actually work on a trail

Think light, dry and easy to eat while walking:

- Dates, almonds, peanuts, roasted chana
- Bananas (the best natural fuel on a short trek)
- Chikki, jaggery pieces, glucose biscuits
- A simple sandwich or thepla roll for the summit break

Skip anything greasy, creamy or heavy. A big plate of misal before the climb feels great for ten minutes and terrible for the next two hours.

And please carry a separate bag for your wrapper and bottle. Whatever you bring up the hill comes down with you.

## First Aid and Personal Health: Small Kit, Big Peace of Mind

You do not need a hospital in your bag. A small pouch covers most weekend situations:

- Band-aids and a small roll of crepe bandage
- Antiseptic wipes or liquid
- Pain-relief spray or balm
- ORS sachets
- Any personal medicines you take regularly, with a basic dose note

If you have any health condition, tell the event organiser before the trek, not on the trail. This is also part of our [safety guidelines](/safety-guidelines): disclose relevant health conditions to organisers beforehand, bring necessary medication, and do not participate if you are feeling unwell. Skipping one weekend is always better than being the reason a group has to turn back.

## Phone, Power and Documents

Mobile network in the Sahyadris is patchy. Treat it as unreliable from the start.

- **Charge everything the night before.** A power bank that is half full is a power bank you did not charge.
- **Download an offline map** of the area on Friday evening, while you still have good signal.
- **Switch the phone to battery saver** and use a proper torch for pre-dawn starts instead of draining the phone.
- **Keep your ID and ticket accessible**, along with a small amount of cash in small notes for tea, parking and village stalls where UPI may not work.
- **Write one emergency contact on a slip of paper** and keep it in your bag.

Our safety guidelines also ask you to tell a friend or family member where you are going, share event details such as location, time and expected return, and keep your phone charged and with you. It takes one WhatsApp message. Send it.

## Winter Is Trek Season: What Changes From November Onwards

Now is a good time to start planning. With the monsoon behind us, the trails around Pune are drying out, the sky clears up, and many forts become far more pleasant to climb. Several trek listings for the region describe winter as the preferred season for routes such as Harishchandragad, while easy forts like Lohagad and Rajmachi stay popular through the cooler months.

What this means for your packing:

- **Early mornings are cold at height.** Carry a light layer even if Pune feels warm at 8 AM.
- **Days are dry, which means dust.** A buff or cloth helps.
- **Sunrise treks need a torch.** Starting in the dark is common, and a headlamp keeps your hands free.
- **Sun is still strong by late morning.** Sunscreen and a cap are not optional.

## Which Trek Should You Pick? A Quick Guide for Pune Beginners

Difficulty is relative to your fitness, the weather and the crowd on the day, so treat this table as a starting point and confirm details with your event organiser before you commit.

If this is your first trek, start with Sinhagad or Lohagad. If you have done two or three easy forts and want a real challenge, Harishchandragad and Kalsubai are the usual next steps, ideally with an experienced group.

## Solo Trekkers and Women: Practical Safety That Actually Helps

A lot of people in Pune are new to the city, working long hours in Hinjewadi, Kharadi or Baner, and do not have a ready trekking gang. That is perfectly fine. Trekking with strangers is one of the oldest ways to build a social circle, and it works precisely because everyone has a shared task.

A few habits make solo trekking safer and more comfortable. These are drawn from our own safety guidelines for events:

1. **Stay with the group at all times.** Do not wander off for photos on unmarked ledges.
2. **Inform the organiser if you need to step away.** Even for a short break.
3. **Share your live location with a trusted person** for the duration of the event.
4. **Arrange your own transport** to and from the meeting point instead of relying on someone you just met.
5. **Share only what you are comfortable sharing.** Your first name is enough.
6. **Trust your instincts.** If someone or something makes you uncomfortable, tell the organiser right away. You do not need to explain yourself twice.

For women attending their first adventure event, our guidance is to choose daytime options where possible and, if the event allows it, bring a friend. None of this is meant to scare you. It is meant to make your first trek about the view and the conversation, not about worry.

Emergency numbers worth saving before any outdoor event: **112** (emergency), **100** (police), **108** (ambulance) and **1091** (women's helpline).

## Trail Etiquette: Small Habits That Keep the Sahyadris Clean

Forts near Pune see heavy footfall every weekend, and the signs are visible in the plastic wrappers that collect near the summit. A few rules:

- Carry back every piece of waste you carry up
- Do not play loud music on the trail
- Do not light fires or smoke near dry grass
- Stay on the marked path to reduce erosion
- Respect heritage structures: no graffiti, no climbing on ruined walls
- Skip alcohol entirely on trek days. Heights and dehydration do not mix with it

## Seven Packing Mistakes We See Again and Again

1. **Carrying too many clothes.** One spare T-shirt is plenty for a day trek.
2. **Relying on phone torch alone.** It drains your battery quickly.
3. **Skipping breakfast** and hoping for the best by midday.
4. **Wearing brand-new shoes.** Always break them in first.
5. **Not telling anyone where you are going.** It takes ten seconds.
6. **Ignoring the weather forecast** on the morning of the trek.
7. **Treating a fort as a picnic spot** and leaving litter behind.

## Frequently Asked Questions

### What should I carry for a one-day trek near Pune?
Water (1.5 to 2 litres), light snacks, a charged phone and power bank, a torch, personal medicines, a small first aid kit, sunscreen, a cap, and a thin outer layer. Pack the master checklist above and you are covered.

### Which is the easiest trek near Pune for a first-timer?
Sinhagad, Lohagad and Rajmachi are commonly suggested for beginners. The right choice depends on your fitness and whether you prefer a short climb or a longer walk with a camping option.

### Can girls go on a weekend trek in Pune alone?
Many women attend group treks solo. Choose an organised group with verified participants, follow standard precautions such as sharing your live location, and tell the organiser if anything feels off. Our events follow a zero-harassment policy.

### What should I wear for a Pune trek in winter?
Dry-fit T-shirt, stretchable bottoms, shoes with grip, and a light windcheater for the cold start. Avoid jeans and cotton hoodies.

### Is it okay to go trekking without any experience?
Yes, if you choose an easy trail, go with a group and carry the basics. Our events are built around beginners, and many attendees show up alone.

### How do I make friends on a trek?
Be on time, help someone with water or a snack when needed, and stay for the post-trek chai. Shared effort does most of the talking for you.
